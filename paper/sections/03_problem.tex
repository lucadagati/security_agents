\section{Problem Definition}
\label{sec:problem}

Let $\mathcal{A}_t$ and $\mathcal{D}_t$ be finite populations of attacker
and defender agents at discrete episode $t=1,\ldots,T$. Agents interact
through a partially observable cyber environment $\mathcal{E}$ for at most
$H$ turns per episode. An agent is a tuple
\begin{equation}
  A_i \;=\; \bigl(M_i,\, G_i,\, \mathcal{T}_i,\, \mathcal{C}_i,\,
  K_i,\, \pi_i,\, \mu_i\bigr),
  \label{eq:agent}
\end{equation}
where $M_i$ is the underlying model (heuristic or LLM), $G_i$ a
high-level objective, $\mathcal{T}_i$ the structured tools the
environment exposes, $\mathcal{C}_i$ the implied capabilities,
$K_i$ the knowledge revealed so far, $\pi_i$ the policy, and $\mu_i$
memory. The environment defines $\mathcal{T}_i$; the experimenter
defines $G_i$; $\pi_i$ is \emph{not} a hard-coded attack or defence
script~\cite{yao2023react}. We distinguish \emph{capability} (what the
agent can do), \emph{objective} (what it wants) and \emph{strategy}
(how it chooses to get there) so that unexpected behaviour can be
studied without adding tools.

Adaptation is classified into three levels: \textbf{L0 (static)}, where
$\pi_i$ and $\mu_i$ do not change; \textbf{L1 (episodic)}, where
$\pi_i$ may adapt within an episode but memory is wiped at the boundary;
and \textbf{L2 (persistent)}, where a compact reflection of each episode
is retained in $\mu_i$. Populations may also communicate through a
time-varying directed graph $G_t=(V_t,E_t)$~\cite{park2023generative};
coalitions are detected \emph{post hoc} from reciprocal same-role
traffic~\cite{abdelnabi2024cooperation}, never instructed explicitly.

We address five research questions: how security behaviour evolves under
repeated interaction (RQ0); how adaptation affects effectiveness (RQ1);
which behaviours emerge without being programmed (RQ2); whether
interaction produces measurable co-evolution (RQ3); and whether
populations converge, oscillate or enter an arms race (RQ5). Population
scaling and communication (RQ4) are part of the experimental programme
but not the focus of the present evaluation. A behaviour $b$ is
\emph{emergent} when it is not encoded in objectives or declared policy
class yet arises consistently from interaction; stable post-hoc
observation of $b$ is a candidate for emergence, not a proof of it.
