\section{Experimental Environment}
\label{sec:environment}

The environment models a layered enterprise network
(Fig.~\ref{fig:network}). An external attacker faces a gateway, service
hosts and a protected data store. The attacker wins by exfiltrating from
the store; the defender wins by detecting, containing and recovering first.

\input{figures/network}

\paragraph{Objectives and observations.}
The attacker objective is to exfiltrate from the data store at minimum cost;
the defender objective is to detect, isolate and recover before exfiltration.
Attackers observe known hosts (reachability, footholds, vulnerabilities),
footholds, credentials and turn budget; they do not see defender actions or
unprobed hosts. Defenders observe per-host suspicion, monitoring level,
isolation state and alert history on monitored hosts; they do not see attacker
intent or off-network activity. Both sides receive schema-validated action
outcomes each turn.

\paragraph{Tools and kill chain.}
Agents interact through a fixed catalogue of structured offensive and
defensive tools (reconnaissance through exfiltration; monitoring through
ingress control). Each tool has a resource cost and noise profile; parameters
are schema-validated before taking effect. Exfiltration requires composing a
multi-step chain under partial observability: reconnaissance, foothold,
optional credential access, lateral movement, then exfiltration. Transition
legality is enforced by the environment API, not by agent scripts. Tool
definitions and JSON schemas are maintained in the open-source artefact
(\hyperref[sec:code]{Code Availability}).

\paragraph{Partial observability and detection.}
Attackers begin with no host knowledge; defenders observe suspicion only on
monitored hosts. A noisy action on host $h$ adds to accumulated suspicion
$\sigma_h$; after each turn an automatic detector fires with probability
$1-e^{-\lambda \sigma_h}$ (we use $\lambda=0.12$ in all reported runs).
Suspicion and monitoring intensity decay by factor $0.85$ per turn. The
\texttt{wait} action adds no noise, so pauses after exploits let $\sigma_h$
decay even though no objective rewards waiting. Table~\ref{tab:sim-params}
lists the remaining simulator defaults.

\paragraph{Tool costs and noise.}
Table~\ref{tab:tool-costs} lists representative offensive tools; defensive
tools follow the same pattern (monitor and investigate are low noise;
isolate and decoy are high cost). Episode \emph{attacker\_cost} and
\emph{defender\_cost} in the telemetry are cumulative tool costs plus
detection penalties; \emph{detection\_cost} counts defender effort triggered
by alerts. Detection timing follows the law in the previous paragraph on
every turn after actions resolve; there is no separate ``scan phase''.

\paragraph{Backends and defaults.}
All reported experiments use the in-memory simulator for reproducibility; an
optional Kubernetes backend implements the same interface. Default instances
use three service hosts, two vulnerabilities per host, twenty turns per
episode, and no inter-agent communication unless noted in the extended experimental
matrix (supplementary material).
