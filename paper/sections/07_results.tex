\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

We report the heuristic adaptation ladder (H1, H2; RQ0--RQ1, RQ3, RQ5) and
the language-model campaign (H4, H7 exploratory) under the protocol of
Section~\ref{sec:method}. RQ2/H5 (emergence vs.\ $B_i^{\mathrm{enc}}$) are not
evaluated: pacing tags are declared in $B_A^{\mathrm{enc}}$ and produced by an
explicit cooldown rule in the policy (Section~\ref{sec:method-policies}).

\subsection{Heuristic adaptation ladder}
\label{sec:results-ladder}
Table~\ref{tab:ladder}, Fig.~\ref{fig:ladder} and Table~\ref{tab:cep-adj}
summarise all nine one-versus-one cells ($3{\times}3$ attacker/defender
adaptation levels) over six seeds with forty episodes each. Static attackers are
detected in $98.3{\pm}1.0\%$ of episodes with low ASR ($0.029$--$0.046$)
regardless of defender adaptation. H2 is \emph{not} supported on DR: static/static
and static/adaptive both yield $\mathrm{DR}=0.983{\pm}0.010$ (paired
$t_{5}=0.00$, $p=1.00$, $d=0.00$; Table~\ref{tab:stats-tests}). When the
\emph{attacker} adapts (L1 or L2), mean DR falls to $0.554$--$0.562$ and ASR
rises modestly to $0.067$--$0.092$. H1 is supported on detection: adaptive/static lowers DR by
$0.425$ relative to static/static (paired diff.\ $=+0.425$ with static as minuend;
$t_{5}=20.82$, $p<0.001$, $d=10.04$; Table~\ref{tab:stats-tests}).

The dominant evasion mechanism is programmed stealth pacing: adaptive attackers
insert low-noise \texttt{wait} turns after noisy exploits so suspicion decays
(Section~\ref{sec:method-policies}). This behaviour is declared in
$B_A^{\mathrm{enc}}$ and implemented by the cooldown rule; we do not treat it
as emergent.

\paragraph{Parameter dynamics (RQ5).}
Table~\ref{tab:dynamics} reports \emph{parameter} dynamics classes based on
stealth/vigilance only. Static/static controls are stable in all six seeds
($6/6$); adaptive and persistent attacker cells are predominantly
non-convergent ($5$--$6/6$ per cell). Full-signature labels (including action-mix
features) mark almost every run as non-convergent and do not discriminate L0
controls; we therefore do not use them for primary claims. Fig.~\ref{fig:arms}
shows parameter change on one representative P/P run; Fig.~\ref{fig:episode-dynamics}
plots rolling DR and attacker stealth on the same run.

\subsection{Language-model evaluation}
\label{sec:results-llm}
Pure LLM policies frequently fail to complete the offensive chain; we
therefore report hybrid B3--B5 cells (Section~\ref{sec:method-policies}).

Table~\ref{tab:llm} and Fig.~\ref{fig:hybrid-asr} summarise six seeds with
thirty episodes each. B4 reaches the highest mean ASR ($0.211{\pm}0.040$);
B5 remains low ($0.044{\pm}0.028$, CI overlaps zero only at the lower bound).
B3 never exfiltrates ($\mathrm{ASR}=0$). H4 is not supported. DR is high for
B3 and B4 ($0.939$ and $0.856$) for distinct reasons (noisy probes vs.\ longer
chains). B4 exceeds B3 on ASR (paired $t_{5}=-10.30$, $p<0.001$, Table~\ref{tab:stats-tests}).
Attacker gate fractions: B3=$0.62{\pm}0.02$, B4=$0.52{\pm}0.02$, B5=$0.67{\pm}0.01$
(Section~\ref{sec:method-policies}).
