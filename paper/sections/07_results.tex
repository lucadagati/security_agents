\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

We report a multi-seed heuristic adaptation ladder and a complementary
language-model evaluation on the same environment. Only adaptation level
and policy class vary within each study.

\subsection{Heuristic adaptation ladder}
\label{sec:results-ladder}
Table~\ref{tab:ladder} and Fig.~\ref{fig:ladder} summarise the
one-versus-one factorial (six seeds, $40$ episodes each). Static attackers
are detected in $98.3{\pm}1.0\%$ of episodes and rarely exfiltrate,
whether the defender adapts or not. Episodic adaptation on the attacker
side roughly halves detection ($\mathrm{DR}=0.558{\pm}0.047$) and raises
success (H1). The dominant mechanism is stealth pacing: after a noisy
exploit the attacker inserts low-noise pauses so suspicion decays, a
behaviour the static policy never selects.

\input{figures/ladder_bars}

CEP is lowest for static/static matchups and highest when both sides adapt
(Fig.~\ref{fig:cep-ladder}). Persistent runs are typically classified as
\emph{arms races}: late-episode signature change exceeds early-episode
change (Fig.~\ref{fig:arms}). On representative persistent runs, rolling
detection falls while attacker stealth and defender vigilance trend upward
(Fig.~\ref{fig:episode-dynamics}), the H2 pattern of suppression followed
by strategic shift rather than convergence.

Post-hoc tagging found behaviours not listed in the configured objectives,
including delayed attack, strategic patience, deception and decoy
deployment (H5). We do not claim general intelligence; we claim these
patterns were not scripted yet arose consistently across seeds.

\subsection{Language-model evaluation}
\label{sec:results-llm}
Pure LLM policies frequently stall in reconnaissance or misuse capabilities
(for example, attempting credential harvest on hosts that hold none). We
therefore report hybrid policies alongside a pure-LLM ablation
(Table~\ref{tab:llm}). Episodic hybrid memory (B4) is the only primary
cell with a clearly positive mean ASR ($0.139{\pm}0.052$;
Fig.~\ref{fig:hybrid-asr}); static hybrid (B3) never exfiltrates and
persistent hybrid (B5) remains near the floor. H4 is \emph{not} supported:
episodic memory outperforms persistent memory in this setting.

Supplementary conditions (Table~\ref{tab:llm_ablation},
Fig.~\ref{fig:llm-ablation}) show non-monotonic model scale, asymmetric
matchups (heuristic attack against hybrid defence reaches the highest
mean ASR), and low success on long-horizon and $3{\times}3$ cells.
Fig.~\ref{fig:action-mix} shows how step-level action frequencies compose
the hybrid attacker's behaviour on a representative B4 run. H7 is
supported: nominal capability does not predict co-evolutionary outcomes
without the appropriate memory regime and control structure.

In summary, co-evolution and emergence are established at one-versus-one
under heuristic policies with confidence intervals. Language-model agents
show the same dependence on adaptation regime, but only when hybrid
control structure permits repeated completion of the offensive chain.
Population scaling (RQ4), ESR (H3) and coalition dynamics (H6) remain
future work.
