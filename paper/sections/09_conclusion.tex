\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We studied co-evolutionary security as a property of repeated attack--defence
interaction under declared adaptation regimes, not of an isolated agent
evaluated once. A heuristic adaptation ladder shows that adaptive attackers
evade detection static attackers cannot, that defender adaptation alone does
not reduce detection against a static attacker (H2 not supported), and that
persistent matchups exhibit sustained non-convergent strategy change rather
than convergence to equilibrium. A complementary language-model campaign shows
that hybrid episodic agents reach the highest ASR while static and persistent
hybrids stay at or near the floor; outcomes depend on memory regime and hybrid
gate structure (exploratory model-scale ablations in Table~\ref{tab:llm_ablation}).

Open questions on population scale, interaction-induced risk and coalitions
are specified in the extended experimental matrix. The central methodological
lesson is to evaluate agentic security under stated adaptation assumptions on
both sides: \emph{do not freeze the opponent.}
