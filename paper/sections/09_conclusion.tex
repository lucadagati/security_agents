\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We studied co-evolutionary security as a property of repeated attack--defence
interaction under declared adaptation regimes, not of an isolated agent
evaluated once. A heuristic adaptation ladder shows that adaptive attackers
evade detection static attackers cannot, persistent matchups enter arms races,
and unencoded behaviours such as patience and delayed attack appear from
interaction. A complementary language-model campaign shows that hybrid
episodic agents can reach measurable success while static and persistent
hybrids cannot, so outcomes depend on memory regime and control structure
more than on model size alone.

Open questions on population scale, interaction-induced risk and coalitions
are specified in the E3--E8 matrix. The central methodological lesson is to
evaluate agentic security under stated adaptation assumptions on both sides:
\emph{do not freeze the opponent.}
